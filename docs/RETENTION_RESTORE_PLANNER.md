# Read-only retention/restore planner

Pure offline calculations in `retention_restore_planner.py`, based on the November 19 audit and approved operating rules. It is **not imported by the application** and has no database/network/clock/filesystem calls. The optional CLI reads JSON from stdin and prints JSON to stdout. No account, allowance, payment, estimate, image, draft or input record is modified; no access enforcement, deletion or jobs are implemented.

```sh
python3 scripts/plan_retention_restore.py < fictional-inventory.json > advisory-plan.json
python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -p test_retention_restore_planner.py -v
```

## Explicit input contract

Top level: `schema:1`, timezone-aware `now`, `mode:inventory|restore`, `records` list, `deletion_ledger`. Restore also requires original `snapshot_at`, not later than now. Invalid envelope/identity rejects the entire plan; invalid record/deadline facts produce unknown/HOLD. All dates normalize to UTC with microsecond output. No inferred account-creation, login, import, revision-edit or billing clocks.

Each record has business-scoped stable `id`, `business_id`, `kind:estimate|revision|imagery|draft`. Identity is unique within a business. Duplicate inventories HOLD, even if identical: reconcile actual copies first. Estimates are roots; revisions require an estimate parent; imagery requires estimate/revision parent. `parent_id` resolves only within the same business. Missing parents, invalid relationships and cycles quarantine the affected descendants. The planner can reconcile only supplied inventory; it cannot prove inventory completeness or discover missing storage/cache/object copies.

Estimate/revision records explicitly select `scope:full|lightweight`; imagery is always full. Supply separate IDs/inventory records for separately retained scopes and an explicit relationship graph. Full composite content includes lightweight fields: deleting lightweight content also suppresses a composite full record. Full-only deletion leaves a separately inventoried lightweight record retained, but still suppresses full imagery/revisions linked through that parent. Parent scopes held/suppressed also quarantine descendants conservatively; this is a proposed hold, not physical destruction of longer-promised content.

Each non-draft record supplies `policy` bound to the same `business_id`, nonempty `provenance`, and the existing standalone evaluator's explicit `period`, `retention`, optional `reactivation` fields (see STANDALONE_POLICY_EVALUATOR.md). The planner reuses its `access` calculation only; no entitlement calculation or account mutation. Period requires id/start/end/payment_status; retention requires paid_access_end, legacy_full, legacy_lightweight, full_present, lightweight_present. Legacy promises are `status:none|known|unknown`; known includes an aware deadline. Known and confirmed-absent promises require provenance; missing facts cannot masquerade as `none`. Full-present requires lightweight-present. Full/lightweight protection remains separate. Optional `recorded_expiry` must agree with the calculated protected expiry; conflict HOLDs rather than choosing a convenient date. Nonempty `exceptions` HOLD for scoped review, never automatic customer exposure.

Approved base expiry is paid-access end + **120 elapsed 24-hour days**. Paid access ends exactly at its supplied end; read-only lasts while now < protected expiry. At exact expiry the scope is expired. Known longer promises win; shorter promises cannot shorten the base. Unknown legacy deadlines stay unknown, including during an active paid period. Historical six-month/full and one-year/lightweight promises require their actual original deadlines/provenance; the planner does not invent their clocks. Ongoing active-subscriber retention remains an unmapped policy schedule; active KEEP is not a newly invented deletion schedule or promise of indefinite storage. Successful reactivation can retain eligible, still-present longer-promised work after day 120; it cannot undo tombstones or promise recovery of deleted data.

Drafts supply `principal`, `last_accepted_at`, `clock_provenance`, explicit boolean `closed`, and explicit `save_operation` (stable ID or null). Optional expires_at must equal last accepted write + **86,400 seconds**. `last_retry_at`, creation/login/import times do not refresh this clock. Accepted new/updated writes refresh it upstream; identical lost-ack retries do not. Exact expiry is expired. Closed drafts never reopen; preserve closed IDs/save-operation identities so a future restore reconciliation does not revive them. Unknown closed/clock facts HOLD. No timestamps or tombstones are refreshed by planning. Actual physical cleanup remains opportunistic in the existing application, not a promise of erasure exactly at 24 hours.

## Deletion ledger and restore quarantine

Ledger inputs require `authoritative:true`, `complete_through` exactly equal to the explicit planning `now`, explicit `covered_business_ids`, `entries` list (empty only when coverage attests no deletion), and for restore `independent_of_snapshot:true`. This strict equality is an explicit coverage fence, not an invented provider refresh interval. Stale/future coverage, missing authority, a ledger from only the same old snapshot, or absent business coverage remain unresolved. These are caller attestations, **not authentication, proof of completeness or durable ledger implementation**. An eventual integration must obtain independently protected canonical evidence through the proposed release instant; no customer access can safely rely on client-supplied booleans.

Entries: event_id, business_id, record_id, kind (`deleted` or `draft_closed`), scope (`all`, `full`, `lightweight`), positive integer sequence, effective_at, provenance. Events cannot postdate coverage/now or address an unattested business. Same business/event ID with identical facts is idempotent; conflicting duplicates/sequences HOLD. Order is not trusted. Deleted record IDs are scoped by business and propagate through related revisions/media. Draft-closed events cannot apply to estimate records with coincident IDs. Missing or stale deletion history NEVER means permission to expose a restored record.

Outputs distinguish `retention_action` from `restore_action`. In inventory mode `proposed_action` describes retention; unresolved deletion coverage still sets restore_action HOLD and quarantine_required true. In restore mode proposed_action is the conservative restore action. KEEP/READ-ONLY are advisory candidates only; SUPPRESS-ON-RESTORE and HOLD require quarantine. Reasons, UTC deadline/provenance, review flag and applicable event IDs accompany each item. No output grants access. Even a plan with no known quarantine issues requires independent release review; it does not actually unquarantine a restore.
